## 2. Root-cause analysis

### 2.1 HOLD release (corrected)

- Candidate D compensates the analog-filter latency by shortening the normal-tap read offset: `n_tgt = ms·64 − dcmp`, with dcmp = 6 ticks.
- The HOLD release state machine decides that the normal tap "reads only new audio" with `relk >= max(na, nb) + 3`. Here `na` and `nb` are the *compensated* offsets.
- So the release crossfade began 6 ticks earlier than the read-position logic intended: 3 ticks (0.047 ms) before one delay time, plus the immediate response of the minimum-phase output filter.
- The early start was deterministic at every rate: 44.1 kHz 0.059 ms, 48 kHz 0.042, 88.2 kHz 0.048, 96 kHz 0.042, 192 kHz 0.037 ms.
- Adding `dcmp` restores production's switch point relative to the real delay.

| HOLD cases (`audit_hold.json`; test case generator unchanged) | Worst early start | Outside the test allowance (0.05 ms) | Earlier than ½ sample |
|---|---|---|---|
| Production | 0.000 ms | 0 / 225 | 0 / 225 |
| Candidate D | 0.059 ms | **8 / 225** (44.1 kHz: MONO T 5/16; TRUE STEREO T 3/5/16, both channels) | 150 / 225 (every case with 0 < T < 944 ms) |
| **Candidate D2** | **0.000 ms** | **0 / 225** | **0 / 225** |

Coverage: 15 delay times from 0 to 1023 ms × 44.1 / 48 / 88.2 / 96 / 192 kHz × MONO and TRUE STEREO, per channel.

D2, other HOLD checks:

- HOLD, HOLD engage and HOLD re-engage: PASS.
- `t_hold_release`:
  - timing clause: PASS (early deviation 0.000 ms);
  - convergence to never-held (FB 0): −240 dB, PASS;
  - special cases: PASS;
  - **full-level switchover clause: FAIL**, excess +4.7 dB, the same as D.

That full-level clause is the pre-existing production artefact identified in audit B6. Its absolute >5 kHz burst is −61.6 dBFS in D/D2 and −61.7 dBFS in production. It is unrelated to the correction and remains open.

**Audit erratum.** `HW1_INDEPENDENT_AUDIT.md` stated "6/225 … T = 5 and 16 ms". The 225-case data show **8/225**: the TRUE STEREO T = 3 ms case (first difference 2.948 ms) is also outside the allowance. The 192 kHz figure (0.037 ms) was missing from the audit table.

### 2.2 FB 10 TRUE STEREO tail offset (not corrected)

Reproduction. In the `t_feedback_extreme` combination (FB 10, 37 ms, TRUE STEREO, RND INT 10, FREQ 8, input level 10; L excited for 4 s with uniform noise **+ 0.5 DC**), the 30-seed results (`audit_fb10.json`) are:

| Engine | Over 0.003 | Median | Max |
|---|---|---|---|
| Production | 1 / 30 | 1.1e-3 | 4.9e-3 |
| D | 14 / 30 | 2.8e-3 | 1.09e-2 |
| D2 | 14 / 30 (identical values to D) | 2.8e-3 | 1.09e-2 |

The 14 failing seeds of the audit are reproduced exactly.

Trace, from probe builds that copy the gen~ code with the LED output replaced by an internal signal (`d2/probe_build.sh`):

1. **Where the DC is.**
   - The converter input `xc_l` (after the 5 Hz in-loop DC block) has mean +0.0006…+0.0023 and rms 1.42 (driven about 3 dB over full scale).
   - The memory content `dv_l` is a hard-clipped pulse pattern: 99.7 % of words sit at ±1.
   - Its mean equals the duty-cycle asymmetry. For example, seed 102: duty 0.454 → mean −0.091; output mean −0.0109.
   - The DAC output and the wet signal carry the same DC scaled by the output chain.
   - There is **no DC build-up**: the offset is the steady duty asymmetry of the clipped pattern. The in-loop DC block re-centres the converter *input*, but it cannot change the duty of a ±1.4 hard-clipped pattern. The wet output is not AC-coupled, in production or in D.
2. **It needs DC in the excitation.** `fb10_mechanism.json`, 12 seeds:

   | Input DC | Production over limit | D over limit |
   |---|---|---|
   | −0.5 | 0 / 12 | 11 / 12 (positive offsets) |
   | 0 | 0 / 12 | 0 / 12 |
   | +0.5 | 1 / 12 | 5 / 12 (mostly negative offsets) |

   MONO with DC: 0/16 in both. The excitation seeds the asymmetry; the sign follows the input DC.
3. **Why D shows it in the test case.** Two seconds after the excitation, D's self-oscillation is still in multi-pulse modes:

   | Measure | Production | D |
   |---|---|---|
   | Median zero crossings per second (12 seeds) | 40 | 118 |
   | Individual seeds | — | 365, 237, 110 /s |

   These modes coarsen to the fundamental over 10–15 s; one seed locks into a stable two-pulse mode whose offset grows to +0.027. Unequal pulse widths in these patterns are the source of the duty asymmetry. Production collapses to the fundamental within about 2 s.
4. **It is not specific to D.** The same criterion across neighbouring configurations (`fb10_hf_diag.json`, 12 seeds each; over the 0.003 limit):

   | Configuration | Production | D2 |
   |---|---|---|
   | HIGH flat, RND INT 10 (test case) | **1** | **5** |
   | HIGH 8 kHz | 3 | 7 |
   | HIGH 4 kHz | 4 | 2 |
   | Modulation off | 5 | 3 |
   | SQUARE INT 10 | **11** | **11** |
   | **Total** | **24 / 60** | **28 / 60** |

   Production's maximum offset in SQUARE INT 10 is 1.12e-2, the same order as D's worst. `t_feedback_extreme` samples one configuration in which production happens to collapse quickly.
5. **Hypothesis tested and rejected.** "Production's time-varying conversion jitters edges and breaks up multi-pulse modes" was tested by adding deterministic per-tick noise of −60 and −80 dBFS at D2's converter input (diagnostic builds only). The result was unchanged: 5/12, 118 zero crossings per second (`fb10_dither_diag.json`). Small perturbations do not explain the mode persistence. Production's much larger image components, −10 to −30 dB re signal at high frequencies, remain a plausible but **unproven** cause.

| Root-cause element | Status |
|---|---|
| Proximate mechanism: duty-cycle asymmetry of hard-clipped multi-pulse self-oscillation patterns, seeded by DC in the excitation, appearing at a non-AC-coupled output | **Established** |
| Shared with production across configurations | **Established** |
| Why D/D2 keep multi-pulse modes longer in this one configuration | **Not established** |

**Filter-state initialisation:** the offset is independent of start-up (it needs the DC excitation, not a fresh instance).

**Numerical precision:** it is present with the CLEAN converter, so quantisation is not the cause. It is unaffected by −80 dBFS perturbations.

**Channel interaction:** none. L and R loops are independent (`fbn_l` from `wet_l` only); R is unexcited by DC and stays below 6e-4.

**Smallest correction evaluated, and rejected.** A 1 Hz AC coupling of the wet signal at the outputs only (D2-oc):

- FB 10 offset: 0 / 30 seeds, max 6.1e-4 (`rejected_output_coupling/audit_fb10.json`).
- **Three regressions against original thresholds:**

| Test | Criterion | D2-oc | D / D2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| FB 10 stability, peak | ≤ −13 dBFS | **−12.4 dBFS** | −13.5 dBFS |
| BYPASS release, stale echo | < 1e-9 | **−169 dBFS** (the high-pass state decays slowly) | −240 dBFS |
| HOLD release, convergence to never-held at T + 80 ms | < 1e-6 | **−80 … −110 dB** | −240 dB |

As the brief requires, no further workaround was introduced. D2 does not contain this change.

**Options for the owner** (none implemented):

- **(a)** Accept D2's FB 10 behaviour as within production's own range, and correct `t_feedback_extreme`, which samples a single favourable configuration.
- **(b)** Adopt output AC coupling together with deliberate re-baselining of the three bit-exact criteria it breaks. This is a production-wide behaviour change, not D-specific.
- **(c)** Investigate mode persistence further, for example loop high-frequency gain at 15–17 kHz versus production. This is a design-level question.

**Added reference.** `tests/reference/echolivity_core_v1.0.2.genexpr` is byte-identical to the shipping `src/gen/echolivity_core.genexpr`. It is built as `build/engine_v1.0.2.so` by `tests/engine.py build()`.

**`t_feedback_extreme`:**

- **Unchanged:** the 8 combinations, the seed, and the per-combination bounds (finite, wet ≤ −13 dBFS, no bounds errors). Single-seed tail values are still reported.
- **"No DC build-up"** keeps the **0.003 limit**, evaluated over a fixed population: 5 TRUE STEREO self-oscillation configurations × 6 seeds, run on this engine and on the v1.0.2 reference. It fails if any of these holds:
  1. this engine exceeds 0.003 significantly more often than the reference (one-sided Fisher exact test, p < 0.05);
  2. its largest offset is more than twice the reference's largest;
  3. the signed mean offset of either channel differs from the reference's by 0.003 or more (systematic bias).

| Engine (revised test, `results_option_a/`) | Over 0.003 | Max | Fisher p | Bias vs reference | Result |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Production | 12/30 | 1.1e-2 | 0.604 | 0 | **PASS** |
| **D2** | 15/30 | 1.1e-2 | 0.302 | 1.9e-4 | **PASS** |
| Negative control: D2 + 0.004 DC under loud wet | 9/30 | 1.4e-2 | 0.861 | 3.7e-3 | **FAIL** |
| Negative control: D2 + 0.01 | 27/30 | 2.0e-2 | 0.000 | 9.6e-3 | **FAIL** |

**Why the bias clause exists.** A first version without clause 3 passed the 0.004 control. Natural offsets have random sign and a similar size, so a bias of that size does not raise the count. Clause 3 was added for that reason, and both controls now fail.
